# StratiGraph documentation

StratiGraph is a set of tools for reading, recording and arguing archaeological
stratigraphy. This is the shelf where their manuals live: one address, one search,
one menu. Each manual sits beside the code it describes, and is published here.

Every manual has two parts, and they are written for two different days: the part
for whoever **works in** the tool, and the part for whoever **builds or installs**
it. You are rarely both at once, and the index of each manual says which is which.

## The manuals

### The node, and its catalogue

The StratiGraph node is the place a team shares: rooms, studies, documents, the
signatures that say who may see what, and the catalogue that publishes what a room
decides to publish. The manual tells how to work in a room and how to run a node of
your own — the first day, the console, the users, the REST interface, the backups.

→ [Read the node manual](https://docs.stratigraph-eccch.eu/projects/node/en/latest/)

### StratiField, the field assistant

The tool that records on site, on a phone or on a laptop with no network, and
synchronises with the node when there is one. It has its own manual because it is its
own tool: what it records, how a field node works, how the field reaches the cloud.

→ [Read the StratiField manual](https://docs.stratigraph-eccch.eu/projects/field/en/latest/)

### The recording templates

The sheets and the vocabularies a team starts from: the stratigraphic unit record, the
axes of status and origin, the modules a discipline adds. Published in English and in
Italian.

→ [Read the templates manual](https://docs.stratigraph-eccch.eu/projects/templates/en/v0.1.0/) · [in italiano](https://docs.stratigraph-eccch.eu/projects/templates/it/v0.1.0/)

## Elsewhere, on purpose

**[The StratiGraph Academy](https://academy.stratigraph-eccch.eu)** is not a manual: it
is where you learn the craft — the competence nodes, the five working phases of an
investigation, the modules of the path. It cites these manuals and never copies them.

**EMStudio, EM Tools, s3Dgraphy, the 3D Survey Collection and Heriverse** belong to the
Extended Matrix ecosystem, which is older than this project and larger than it. Their
manuals stay in their house, [docs.extendedmatrix.org](https://docs.extendedmatrix.org),
and the pages here point at them where a step of the work passes through them. EMStudio's
manual is published in English and Italian:
[EMStudio 1.6](https://docs.extendedmatrix.org/projects/emstudio-doc/en/1.6/) ·
[in italiano](https://docs.extendedmatrix.org/projects/emstudio-doc/it/1.6/).

**The Extended Matrix method** — how a stratigraphic reading is argued, rather than which
button records it — is taught by the EM Academy and described in the language
documentation at [docs.extendedmatrix.org](https://docs.extendedmatrix.org).

## Where these tools come from

StratiGraph — *Knowledge Graphs for Stratigraphy* — is a Horizon Europe Innovation Action
(grant agreement 101232855, 2025–2029), coordinated by CNR-ISPC. It starts from a plain
observation: stratigraphy is the backbone of archaeological and palaeontological reasoning,
and it is still recorded in forms that software can store but cannot argue with. The tools
documented here are the project's answer — a node that holds the work of a team, an
assistant that records in a trench with no network, recording sheets mapped onto a shared
ontology, and under all of them the graph that carries an interpretation from the edge of
an excavation to a published study.

The tools are built to join the **European Collaborative Cloud for Cultural Heritage**: a
node federates with it rather than standing alone, so that a study made in one institution
can be found, cited and reused in another. The project, its partners and its case studies
are at [stratigraph-eccch.eu](https://stratigraph-eccch.eu); the grant is public on
[CORDIS](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/101232855).

The manuals are written beside the code they describe, by the people who write that code.
Where a tool belongs to a partner or to the older Extended Matrix ecosystem, its manual
says so and keeps its own house.

## Citing a manual

Each manual carries the version of the tool it describes, and keeps its older versions
readable: the version menu at the bottom left of every page moves between them. When a
deliverable cites a manual, it cites the version, not `latest`, so that the sentence
quoted today is still the sentence found tomorrow.
